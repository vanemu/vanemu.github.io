FACT: only 1.7% of iOS apps ever make >$10k/mo.

事实：只有 1.7% 的 iOS 应用月收入能超过 1 万美元。

I wanted to understand what those apps have in common, so I took data on 268,117 apps and used Claude's new Dashboards feature to break down the winners.

我想搞清楚这些应用有什么共同点，于是拿了 268,117 个应用的数据，用 Claude 新的 Dashboards 功能拆解赢家。

The method is stupidly simple: start with the apps already making money, work backward from what they're doing, and look for the decisions that keep repeating. What problem do they solve? How quickly can someone understand the product? What happens during onboarding? Where do they put the paywall? How do they acquire customers, and what makes those customers stay?

方法简单到离谱：从已经在赚钱的应用入手，倒推它们在做什么，找出反复出现的决策。它们解决什么问题？别人多快能看懂产品？引导流程里发生了什么？付费墙放在哪里？如何获客，又是什么让客户留下来？

Again, that's 1.7% of ALL iOS apps. And yet, it still represents more than 30,000 apps making serious money. There is plenty to learn from without trying to predict the future or invent an entirely new market.

再说一次，这是全部 iOS 应用里的 1.7%。可即便如此，也仍有超过 3 万个应用在认真赚钱。不必预测未来或发明全新市场，就有大量可学之处。

I'm the kind of person who over-analyzes everything. I look at what successful products sell, how they onboard users, where their traffic comes from, what customers complain about, and why anyone keeps paying after the first month.

我是那种什么都过度分析的人。我会看成功产品卖什么、如何引导用户、流量从哪来、客户抱怨什么，以及为什么有人第一个月之后还会继续付费。

I've also lost $300k+ making expensive mistakes of my own. The annoying part is that most of those mistakes were completely avoidable, which is exactly why I now spend so much time studying what's already working.

我自己也因为昂贵的错误亏过 30 万美元以上。烦人的是，那些错误大多完全可以避免——正因为如此，我现在才花这么多时间研究已经跑通的东西。

If you cannot afford to spend years and hundreds of thousands of dollars learning all of this the painful way, you probably cannot afford to ignore the evidence sitting right in front of you.

如果你花不起数年、数十万美元用痛苦方式学完这一切，你大概也忽略不起眼前摆着的证据。

And this is where Claude Dashboards becomes interesting. Instead of staring at spreadsheets, comparing numbers manually, and trying to remember patterns across hundreds of products, you can connect your data and ask Claude to turn it into a dashboard. It can compare categories, visualize trends, break down performance, and show you the queries behind the numbers.

而这正是 Claude Dashboards 变得有意思的地方。不必盯着表格、手工比数、试图记住几百个产品的模式——你可以接上数据，让 Claude 做成仪表盘。它可以比较品类、可视化趋势、拆解表现，并展示数字背后的查询。

The dashboard can also stay updated as the underlying data changes. That makes it much more useful than doing one giant research session, saving the findings somewhere, and never looking at them again.

仪表盘还能随底层数据变化保持更新。这比做一次巨型调研、把结论存到某处、然后永远不再看，有用得多。

Mobile apps are particularly interesting right now because building used to be the hardest part. You needed Swift, developers, months to get an MVP out, and then you still had to figure out how to get the thing through the App Store.

移动应用眼下特别有意思，因为以前最难的是「做出来」。你需要 Swift、开发者、几个月才能弄出 MVP，然后还得琢磨怎么过 App Store。

That's no longer the same problem. Claude Code, Shipper, and the rest of the AI coding wave have made building dramatically easier. Claude Dashboards helps you understand the data and recognize patterns, while Shipper helps you turn those insights into an actual product.

那已经不是同一个问题了。Claude Code、Shipper，以及整波 AI 编程浪潮，让构建轻松得多。Claude Dashboards 帮你理解数据、识别模式，Shipper 则帮你把洞见变成真正的产品。

The advantage has moved somewhere else: choosing the right idea, building something people want, getting them to pay, and, most importantly, DISTRIBUTION.

优势已经转移到别处：选对点子、做出人们想要的东西、让他们付钱，以及——最重要的——分发（DISTRIBUTION）。

My brother @chddaniel and I have spent years building internet businesses together. Once you study enough successful apps, the same things start showing up. The ideas repeat, the onboarding repeats, the paywalls repeat, and even the marketing formats start looking familiar.

我和弟弟 @chddaniel 一起做互联网生意已经很多年。一旦你研究够多成功应用，同样的东西就会反复出现。点子重复、引导重复、付费墙重复，连营销格式都开始眼熟。

You do not have to reinvent every piece of a successful business. You need to understand why these patterns work and then execute them better.

你不必把成功生意的每一块都重新发明。你需要理解这些模式为何有效，然后执行得更好。

If I were starting from zero today, here's exactly what I would do.

如果我今天从零开始，我会精确地这样做。

1. START WITH A PROBLEM PEOPLE ALREADY WANT SOLVED

1. 从人们已经想解决的问题开始

Founders love saying that ideas don't matter and execution is everything. I don't really buy that. Execution obviously matters, but picking a weak idea makes every other part of building a business unnecessarily difficult.

创始人爱说点子不重要、执行才是一切。我并不完全买账。执行当然重要，但选了弱点子，会让创业的其他部分都变得没必要地艰难。

It's called B2C for a reason: business to consumer. Yet somehow, the consumer is usually the part founders spend the least time thinking about.

之所以叫 B2C，是有原因的：business to consumer。可消费者往往是创始人想得最少的那一块。

Before worrying about branding, features, UI, or which AI model you're going to use, figure out exactly who is supposed to pull out their card and pay for your app. More importantly, understand why they would care enough to do that in the first place.

在操心品牌、功能、UI，或要用哪个 AI 模型之前，先弄清到底谁该掏出卡来为你的应用付费。更重要的是，弄清他们为什么会在乎到愿意付钱。

If I were starting with limited time and money, I'd focus on problems people already think about every day. Losing weight, looking better, screen-time addiction, getting over an ex, sleeping better, quitting an addiction, relationships, tracking food, or getting stronger.

如果我时间与金钱都有限，我会聚焦人们每天已经在想的问题。减肥、变好看、屏幕时间成瘾、走出前任、睡得更好、戒瘾、关系、记饮食，或变得更强壮。

These are excellent markets because you don't need to educate people about the problem. They already know it exists. They're searching for solutions on Google, watching TikToks about it, complaining about it to friends, and, most importantly, spending money trying to fix it.

这些是极好的市场，因为你不必教育人们「问题存在」。他们已经知道。他们在 Google 搜解决方案，在 TikTok 上看相关内容，跟朋友抱怨，而且——最重要的——花钱试图解决。

Look at Cal AI, for example. The entire value proposition fits into one sentence: take a photo of your food and instantly track the calories.

以 Cal AI 为例。整个价值主张一句话就够：拍一张食物照片，立刻追踪卡路里。

There's obviously more happening behind the scenes, but the promise is immediately understandable. People want to lose weight, tracking calories helps, and manually logging ingredients and meals is annoying. Cal AI removes that friction.

幕后当然还有更多，但承诺立刻能懂。人们想减肥，记卡路里有帮助，而手工记录食材和餐食很烦。Cal AI 去掉了那份摩擦。

The app reportedly made around $28k in its first month, $115k in its second, and eventually reached roughly $1m in monthly recurring revenue within six months.

据说该应用第一个月大约赚了 2.8 万美元，第二个月 11.5 万，并在六个月内达到约 100 万美元的月经常性收入。

The lesson isn't to open Shipper and build Cal AI #483. It's that a very simple solution attached to a massive, existing desire can become an enormous business.

教训不是打开 Shipper 去做第 483 个 Cal AI。而是：一个非常简单的方案，贴上巨大的、已有的欲望，可以成为庞大生意。

Competition is usually a good sign

竞争通常是好信号

A lot of founders discover competitors and immediately decide they're too late. I'd usually think the opposite, especially when the existing products are already generating meaningful revenue.

很多创始人一发现竞争对手，就立刻觉得自己太晚了。我通常想的相反，尤其当现有产品已经在产生可观收入时。

Steve Hoyek's Journable is a great example. It entered calorie tracking, arguably one of the most competitive categories in consumer apps, and instead of inventing a new market, the team looked at what people disliked about the products they were already using.

Steve Hoyek 的 Journable 是个好例子。它进入卡路里追踪——可以说是消费应用里竞争最激烈的品类之一——团队没有发明新市场，而是去看人们已经在用的产品里讨厌什么。

Many calorie trackers had become complicated and bloated. Journable focused on making the experience simpler, and eventually crossed $100k/month, with most of that revenue reportedly coming from Android.

许多卡路里追踪器变得复杂臃肿。Journable 专注把体验做得更简单，最终突破了每月 10 万美元，据说大部分收入来自 Android。

That last part is interesting on its own, considering how obsessed mobile founders tend to be with iOS.

最后这一点本身就很有意思，考虑到移动创始人往往对 iOS 有多执着。

Competition can be one of the best signals you have. If five apps are solving roughly the same problem and making $20k, $50k, or $100k+ every month, you already have proof that people are willing to pay.

竞争可以是你最好的信号之一。如果有五个应用在解决大致相同的问题，并且每月赚 2 万、5 万或 10 万美元以上，你已经有证据说明人们愿意付钱。

Compare that to an idea nobody has ever touched. Maybe you've discovered an incredible blue ocean. Or maybe nobody built it because nobody actually wants it. I'd much rather have evidence.

对比一个从没人碰过的点子。也许你发现了惊人的蓝海。也可能没人做是因为根本没人想要。我更想要证据。

This is where I'd use Claude Dashboards instead of making assumptions from a few successful examples.

这正是我会用 Claude Dashboards 的地方，而不是从几个成功案例做假设。

Give it a dataset of competing apps, their revenue estimates, categories, pricing, growth history, and whatever acquisition data you can reliably collect. Ask it to compare the winners, identify patterns across categories, and surface the differences between apps that break through and apps that don't.

给它一组竞品应用数据：收入估算、品类、定价、增长历史，以及你能可靠收集到的获客数据。让它比较赢家、识别跨品类模式，并找出突围应用与未突围应用的差异。

The advantage is that you can actually see the data. Instead of Claude simply telling you that subscription apps perform well in a particular niche, you can ask for a breakdown by category, revenue range, or monetization model, and inspect the underlying query.

优势在于你能真正看见数据。Claude 不只是告诉你「订阅应用在某细分表现好」，你可以按品类、收入区间或变现模式要拆解，并检查底层查询。

Before committing to an app, I would want to answer four questions. Are multiple competitors making serious money? How big have the winners become? What do customers hate about the existing products? And, most importantly, where are those companies getting their customers?

在押注一个应用之前，我想回答四个问题。是否有多个竞争对手在认真赚钱？赢家做到了多大？客户讨厌现有产品的什么？以及——最重要的——那些公司从哪里获得客户？

That fourth question matters more than most founders realize. Distribution should influence what you decide to build.

第四个问题比多数创始人意识到的更重要。分发应该影响你决定做什么。

An app that makes sense in a 7-second TikTok demonstration has a completely different growth profile from something that takes three minutes to explain. A product where people naturally share their results has an advantage over one where everything happens privately. A transformation app naturally produces before-and-after content.

一个能在 7 秒 TikTok 演示里说清楚的应用，与需要三分钟才能解释清楚的东西，增长曲线完全不同。人们会自然分享结果的产品，比一切都发生在私下的产品更有优势。改造类应用天然会产生前后对比内容。

All of these things matter before you write your first prompt.

这一切，都在你写下第一个提示词之前就很重要。

Don't spend weeks trying to invent a problem nobody has solved. Find a market where people already spend money, understand why the winners are winning, figure out what users still dislike, and build something meaningfully better, simpler, or different.

别花几周去发明一个没人解决过的问题。找到人们已经在花钱的市场，理解赢家为何赢，弄清用户仍讨厌什么，然后做出明显更好、更简单或不同的东西。

You don't need to reinvent human behavior. Build around behavior that already exists.

你不必重新发明人类行为。围绕已经存在的行为去构建。

2. VALIDATED THE IDEA? BUILD IT FAST.

2. 点子验证过了？那就快建。

Once I'm convinced the market exists, I want the first version in the App Store as quickly as possible. Ideally within 7 days, maybe 14 if the product genuinely needs more work, but definitely not three months.

一旦我确信市场存在，就想尽快把第一版放进 App Store。理想是 7 天内，如果产品确实需要更多工作也许 14 天，但绝不要三个月。

Every extra week you spend building before somebody actually uses the product is another week you might be working on something nobody asked for.

在有人真正使用产品之前，你每多花一周构建，就多一周可能在做没人要的东西。

Your first version really only needs three things: onboarding, monetization, and the core app loop. Everything else can wait until you've got real people using the product and telling you what's missing.

第一版其实只需要三样东西：引导、变现，以及核心应用循环。其余都可以等有了真实用户、他们告诉你缺什么之后再说。

Building apps in 2026 is completely different from how it used to be. With Shipper, you can describe what you want and have AI build the product instead of translating everything into code yourself.

2026 年做应用，和过去完全不同。有了 Shipper，你可以描述想要什么，让 AI 来构建产品，而不必自己把一切翻译成代码。

But that doesn't mean you should type "build me a fitness app" and expect something amazing to come out. The quality of what you build still depends heavily on how well you understand the product in the first place.

但这不意味着你敲一句「给我做一个健身应用」就能期待惊艳结果。你做出的质量，仍然高度取决于你对产品本身理解得有多好。

Before building anything, I would download 5-10 of the best apps in the category and actually use them. Go through the entire experience like a customer rather than a founder trying to copy a few screens.

动手之前，我会下载品类里最好的 5–10 个应用并真正使用。像顾客一样走完整个体验，而不是像创始人一样只想复制几张屏幕。

Screenshot their onboarding, paywall, home screen, core feature, results, and anything that feels unusually polished. Pay attention to what they include, but also to what they've deliberately left out.

截下它们的引导、付费墙、首页、核心功能、结果页，以及任何显得特别打磨的地方。注意它们放了什么，也注意它们刻意没放什么。

Then ask why each decision exists. Why does onboarding ask that particular question? Why does the paywall appear after that specific screen? Why are there only three actions on the home screen? Why does the app present results in that exact way?

然后问每个决策为何存在。引导为什么问那个问题？付费墙为什么出现在那一屏之后？首页为什么只有三个操作？应用为什么以那种方式呈现结果？

You're not trying to steal somebody's design. You're trying to reverse engineer the product decisions underneath it.

你不是在偷别人的设计。你是在逆向工程其下的产品决策。

Claude is useful here, but I'd separate the research into two parts. Use Claude to compare screenshots, reviews, onboarding flows, and the qualitative decisions behind successful apps. Then use Dashboards for the quantitative side: pricing, revenue estimates, conversion data where available, retention, or trends across competitors.

Claude 在这里很有用，但我会把研究分成两部分。用 Claude 比较截图、评价、引导流程，以及成功应用背后的定性决策。再用 Dashboards 做定量一侧：定价、收入估算、可得的转化数据、留存，或竞品趋势。

The important part is knowing what your data actually supports. Claude Dashboards can visualize and compare the data you connect, but it can't magically know a competitor's private conversion rates or retention numbers.

重要的是清楚你的数据实际支持什么。Claude Dashboards 可以可视化并比较你接入的数据，但它无法神奇地知道竞品的私有转化率或留存数字。

Once you've identified the patterns, you can give Shipper significantly better instructions. Don't describe everything with vague words like "modern," "beautiful," or "premium." Show it what you mean.

一旦识别出模式，你就能给 Shipper 好得多的指令。别用「现代」「好看」「高级」这类模糊词描述一切。展示你的意思。

Attach screenshots and visual references. Explain how the user moves from opening the app to completing the main action, what happens after that action, where monetization fits, and how the entire experience should feel.

附上截图和视觉参考。说明用户从打开应用到完成主操作如何移动，动作之后发生什么，变现落在哪里，以及整体体验应该是什么感觉。

AI builders are only as good as the context you give them. Being vague means asking the AI to make dozens of product, UX, and design decisions on your behalf, and then people wonder why their app looks like another generic AI-generated product.

AI 构建器只和你给的上下文一样好。含糊意味着让 AI 替你做几十个产品、UX 和设计决策，然后人们纳闷为什么应用看起来像又一个通用的 AI 生成品。

Don't make it guess.

别让它猜。

My process would be to find the winning apps in the category, break down their onboarding, paywalls, and core loops, decide where I can genuinely improve the experience, and give Shipper the plan alongside the relevant screenshots and visual references.

我的流程会是：找到品类赢家，拆解其引导、付费墙和核心循环，决定我能真正改进体验的地方，把计划连同相关截图和视觉参考交给 Shipper。

Then I'd keep building and iterating until the result feels like something I'd personally want to download and pay for.

然后持续构建和迭代，直到结果感觉像我会亲自想下载并付费的东西。

That last part matters more than people think. A functioning app is not necessarily a finished app, especially when your competitors already have years of polish behind them.

最后这一点比人们以为的更重要。能跑的应用未必是完成的应用，尤其当你的竞争对手已经有多年打磨时。

If the spacing looks wrong, fix it. If the typography makes the product feel cheap, change it. If a screen has 12 different things happening at once, simplify it. And whenever you struggle to explain a design decision, find an app that does it properly and show the AI the reference.

间距不对，就改。字体让产品显得廉价，就换。一屏同时发生 12 件事，就简化。每当你很难解释某个设计决策时，找一个做得对的应用，把参考给 AI 看。

There's basically no reason anymore for vibe coded to automatically mean AI slop. The tools are good enough. You just need enough taste to notice when something isn't right and enough patience to keep improving it.

基本上再也没有理由让「vibe coded」自动等于 AI 垃圾。工具已经够好。你只需要足够的品味去察觉不对劲，以及足够的耐心去持续改进。

For consumer apps, design is particularly important because the UI itself can become a distribution channel. Somebody watches a three-second TikTok showing your app and immediately wants to know what it is. Users screenshot their results and send them to friends. Creators actually want to show the product because it looks good.

对消费应用来说，设计尤其重要，因为 UI 本身可以成为分发渠道。有人看了三秒展示你应用的 TikTok，立刻想知道那是什么。用户截图结果发给朋友。创作者真的想展示产品，因为它好看。

Sometimes Apple even features it. Design isn't just decoration when the product itself is what you're marketing.

有时 Apple 甚至会精选它。当你营销的就是产品本身时，设计不只是装饰。

So yes, BUILD FAST. Just don't confuse speed with shipping whatever the AI gives you after your first prompt.

所以，是的，要快建。只是别把速度等同于把 AI 第一次提示给出的东西直接上线。

Use Shipper to compress months of development into days, and spend some of that saved time making the product feel worth paying for.

用 Shipper 把数月开发压缩成数天，并把省下的一部分时间，花在让产品感觉值得付费上。

3. THE DOWNLOAD IS NOT THE SALE

3. 下载不等于成交

This might be the most important lesson in the entire article, and it's also where I think Claude Dashboards becomes especially useful once you start collecting your own product data.

这可能是整篇文章最重要的一课，也是我认为一旦你开始收集自己的产品数据，Claude Dashboards 特别有用的地方。

By the time somebody reaches your onboarding, you've already accomplished something difficult. They watched your TikTok, stopped scrolling, clicked through, visited the App Store, downloaded your app, and actually opened it.

当有人到达你的引导时，你已经完成了很难的事。他们看了你的 TikTok，停下滑动，点进去，访问 App Store，下载了应用，并且真正打开了它。

Maybe you paid to acquire that person. Maybe you spent hours making the content that got them there. Either way, you now have one job: don't waste their attention.

也许你付钱获来了这个人。也许你花了数小时做内容才把他们带到这里。无论哪种，你现在只有一件工作：别浪费他们的注意力。

Most apps waste it almost immediately with some variation of "Welcome! Here are our 3 amazing features. Please create an account!" Nobody cares about that.

多数应用几乎立刻就浪费掉了，用某种变体说：「欢迎！这是我们 3 个超棒功能。请创建账号！」没人在乎。

Onboarding shouldn't feel like an instruction manual. It should feel like the person is already making progress toward the result they downloaded the app to get.

引导不该像说明书。它该感觉像：这个人已经在朝下载应用想要的结果前进。

Look at @yazio's onboarding. I love their app, and one of the things worth studying is how the experience is built around understanding what the user wants rather than simply listing features.

看看 @yazio 的引导。我喜欢他们的应用，值得研究的一点是：体验围绕理解用户想要什么来构建，而不是简单罗列功能。

Think of onboarding as moving someone through a sequence of beliefs. First, they recognize their problem. Then they feel the app understands it. Then they start believing the product might actually solve it, and finally, they want the result enough to consider paying.

把引导想成带人走过一串信念。首先，他们认出自己的问题。然后感觉应用理解它。接着开始相信产品或许真能解决，最后，他们足够想要结果，以至于考虑付费。

Every screen should contribute to that progression. Some questions personalize the experience, others make the user think about how much the problem affects them, and others introduce proof or create small commitments before asking for a bigger one.

每一屏都应推动这段进程。有些问题个性化体验，有些让用户思考问题对自己影响多大，有些引入证明，或在要求更大承诺前制造小承诺。

Whenever possible, let somebody experience the actual product before showing them the paywall.

只要可能，就让人在看到付费墙之前先体验真正的产品。

If you're building a calorie tracker, don't spend 15 screens explaining how brilliant your scanner is. Let them scan something. If you're making a screen-time app, show people what their existing usage looks like. If you're building a looksmaxing app, give them part of the analysis before asking them to pay for the rest.

如果你在做卡路里追踪器，别花 15 屏解释扫描器有多聪明。让他们扫点东西。如果你做屏幕时间应用，展示人们现有使用状况。如果你做 looksmaxing 应用，先给一部分分析，再要求为其余部分付费。

Ideally, the user reaches the paywall already wanting whatever is on the other side.

理想情况下，用户到达付费墙时，已经想要墙另一边的东西。

Don't invent onboarding from scratch

别从零发明引导

The largest apps in your category have spent years experimenting with questions, screen order, copy, paywalls, prices, and different offers. Some have likely tested hundreds of variations you'll never get to see.

你品类里最大的应用，已经花数年试验问题、屏幕顺序、文案、付费墙、价格和不同优惠。有些很可能测过你永远看不到的上百种变体。

But you can study the version that survived all those experiments, completely free.

但你可以完全免费研究那些实验里最终存活下来的版本。

Again, the goal isn't copying screens without understanding them. It's recognizing recurring decisions. If several successful apps ask similar questions before showing a result, that's worth investigating. If the biggest competitors introduce social proof at roughly the same point, pay attention.

再说一次，目标不是不理解就抄屏幕。而是识别反复出现的决策。如果几个成功应用在展示结果前问类似的问题，就值得调查。如果最大的竞争对手大致在同一点引入社会证明，就要注意。

And if the successful products consistently wait until users receive something personal before displaying the paywall, that's another hypothesis worth testing.

如果成功产品一贯等到用户收到一些个性化内容后再显示付费墙，那又是一个值得测试的假设。

Cal AI is a particularly good example of how far this can go. Its team reportedly ran 123 A/B experiments, tested 160 unique paywall designs, and produced 424 total variants, including 61 meaningful experiments specifically focused on the onboarding paywall.

Cal AI 是能走多远的特别好例子。据说其团队跑了 123 次 A/B 实验，测试了 160 种独特付费墙设计，产出 424 个总变体，其中包括 61 次专门聚焦引导付费墙的有意义实验。

During that period, monthly revenue grew more than 3x, while trial-to-paid conversion improved by 31%.

在那段时间，月收入增长超过 3 倍，试用转付费转化提升了 31%。

That doesn't happen because someone comes up with one genius onboarding experience in an afternoon. It happens because hundreds of relatively small decisions get tested until the metrics improve.

这不是因为有人一个下午想出天才引导体验。而是因为成百上千个相对小的决策被测试到指标改善为止。

Your onboarding should work the same way. Version one isn't your final onboarding. It's hypothesis #1.

你的引导也该如此运作。第一版不是最终引导。它是假设 #1。

And this is where I'd build my first dashboard.

而这正是我会建第一个仪表盘的地方。

Connect your product analytics and subscription data, then have Claude build a view showing how users move through onboarding, where they drop off, how many reach the paywall, how many start trials, and how many become paying customers.

接入你的产品分析和订阅数据，然后让 Claude 建一个视图：用户如何走过引导、在哪里流失、多少人到达付费墙、多少人开始试用、多少人成为付费客户。

Instead of guessing where the problem is, you can visualize the funnel, compare cohorts, and ask Claude why a particular conversion rate changed. Since every chart exposes its underlying query, you can also check what is actually being counted rather than blindly trusting a percentage.

不必猜测问题在哪，你可以可视化漏斗、比较队列，并问 Claude 某个转化率为何变化。因为每张图都暴露底层查询，你也能核对实际在计什么，而不是盲目相信一个百分比。

If onboarding completion drops, investigate the relevant screen. If trial starts are healthy but paid conversion is terrible, investigate the paywall, offer, or trial experience. If people pay but immediately cancel, the problem has probably moved into the product itself.

如果引导完成率下降，调查相关屏幕。如果试用开始健康但付费转化糟糕，调查付费墙、优惠或试用体验。如果人们付了费却立刻取消，问题大概已经移到产品本身。

You don't need to redesign your entire app every week. Sometimes removing a screen, changing one sentence, moving the aha moment earlier, or changing when social proof appears can affect every future customer who downloads the app.

你不必每周重设计整个应用。有时删掉一屏、改一句话、把「啊哈时刻」提前，或改变社会证明出现的时机，就能影响每一个未来下载应用的客户。

The important thing is that you're making decisions based on what people actually do rather than what you think they should do.

重要的是：你基于人们实际做什么做决策，而不是基于你以为他们该做什么。

Your onboarding and paywall are never really finished. They're part of the growth engine, and you should keep improving them as long as you have users coming through.

你的引导和付费墙从来不会真正做完。它们是增长引擎的一部分，只要还有用户进来，你就该持续改进。

4. START DISTRIBUTION BEFORE THE APP IS FINISHED

4. 应用还没做完，就开始分发

Don't wait until Apple approves your app before thinking about marketing. While you're building, return to the competitors you researched earlier, but this time ignore the product itself and focus almost entirely on how they're getting customers.

别等 Apple 批准应用才开始想营销。构建的同时，回到你早先研究的竞品，但这次忽略产品本身，几乎只关注它们如何获客。

Find their TikTok and Instagram accounts. Search their app names to discover which creators are promoting them. Find the founders, look for paid ads, and save every piece of content that seems to perform unusually well.

找到他们的 TikTok 和 Instagram 账号。搜应用名，发现哪些创作者在推广。找到创始人，寻找付费广告，保存每一条表现异常好的内容。

At this stage, you're not trying to prove how creative or original you are. You're trying to understand what the audience and the algorithm have already demonstrated they respond to.

在这个阶段，你不是在证明自己多有创意或多原创。你是在理解受众和算法已经证明会对什么做出反应。

Pay attention to the opening hook, the first frame, how quickly the app is shown, how the product is demonstrated, and the call to action. If a particular format keeps performing well across multiple competing apps, that's valuable research you can use immediately.

注意开场钩子、第一帧、多快展示应用、如何演示产品，以及行动号召。如果某种格式在多个竞品应用上持续表现好，那就是你立刻能用的宝贵研究。

Make your own variations, and make a lot of them.

做你自己的变体，而且要大量做。

The Marketing Hierarchy

营销层级

This is how I think about whether marketing is actually working. There are six tiers, and your goal is to move through every single one.

这是我判断营销是否真正有效的方式。一共六层，你的目标是一层层往上走。

Tier 0: Low views

第 0 层：低播放

Tier 1: Views

第 1 层：播放

Tier 2: Views + Engagement

第 2 层：播放 + 互动

Tier 3: Views + Engagement + Downloads

第 3 层：播放 + 互动 + 下载

Tier 4: Views + Engagement + Downloads + Conversions

第 4 层：播放 + 互动 + 下载 + 转化

Tier 5: Views + Engagement + Downloads + Conversions + Low churn

第 5 层：播放 + 互动 + 下载 + 转化 + 低流失

The tier where you get stuck is usually a very good indication of what you should fix next.

你卡住的那一层，通常很好指示下一步该修什么。

At Tier 0, your content isn't doing its job. Nobody is stopping to watch, so there's very little point worrying about conversion rates or monetization when the opening hook isn't even generating attention.

在第 0 层，你的内容没完成工作。没人停下来看，所以当开场钩子甚至拉不住注意力时，操心转化率或变现几乎没意义。

At Tier 1, you've figured out how to get views, which is useful, but 500k views with almost no downloads isn't necessarily successful marketing. A big number underneath your TikTok doesn't mean much if nobody is interested enough to try the product.

在第 1 层，你搞清楚了如何获得播放，这有用，但 50 万播放几乎没有下载，未必算成功营销。TikTok 下面的大数字，如果没人有兴趣尝试产品，意义不大。

Tier 2 can be even more misleading. You might get thousands of likes, hundreds of comments, and people arguing underneath the post. Maybe you're entertaining, maybe the topic is controversial, or maybe people genuinely enjoyed watching it.

第 2 层甚至更误导。你可能有成千上万点赞、几百条评论，以及帖子下面的争论。也许你很有娱乐性，也许话题有争议，也许人们真的喜欢看。

None of those things automatically mean they want your app. Don't confuse engagement with purchase intent until you've seen it move people farther through the funnel.

这些都不自动意味着他们想要你的应用。在你看到互动把人推得更远穿过漏斗之前，别把互动和购买意图混淆。

At Tier 3, the marketing is finally doing its job. People are watching, understanding what the product does, and downloading it. If thousands of people install the app but hardly anybody pays, the problem is no longer necessarily your content.

到了第 3 层，营销终于在做事。人们在看、理解产品做什么，并下载。如果成千上万的人安装了应用却几乎没人付钱，问题未必还在你的内容。

The marketing brought them in. Now you need to investigate onboarding, monetization, or the product itself.

营销把他们带进来了。现在你需要调查引导、变现或产品本身。

Tier 4 is where everything starts connecting. Views become downloads, and downloads turn into paying customers. Now you've got something that might be worth scaling.

第 4 层是一切开始连上的地方。播放变成下载，下载变成付费客户。现在你有了或许值得放大的东西。

Tier 5 is the actual destination. The content worked, onboarding worked, people paid, and the product delivered enough value that they stayed.

第 5 层才是真正目的地。内容奏效了，引导奏效了，人们付了钱，产品交付了足够价值让他们留下来。

This is exactly the kind of information I'd want visible in Claude Dashboards every day.

这正是我希望每天都能在 Claude Dashboards 里看见的那类信息。

Connect the marketing, attribution, analytics, and revenue data you have available, then ask Claude to build a dashboard that follows performance from the first impression to the paying customer. Break it down by campaign, creative, acquisition channel, and cohort wherever you can reliably attribute the data.

接入你手头的营销、归因、分析和收入数据，然后让 Claude 建一个仪表盘，跟踪从第一次曝光到付费客户的表现。在你能可靠归因的地方，按活动、素材、获客渠道和队列拆解。

The useful part is being able to ask questions like which campaign has the best download-to-paid conversion, whether a spike in installs actually produced retained subscribers, or whether the most viral creative is also generating the most valuable customers.

有用的是能问：哪个活动的下载到付费转化最好，安装激增是否真的带来了留存订阅者，或最病毒的素材是否也在产生最有价值的客户。

And because the dashboard can refresh as the source data changes, you're not rebuilding the same report manually every time you want to make a decision.

而且因为仪表盘可随源数据变化刷新，你不必每次想做决策时都手工重做同一份报告。

This is why I don't care much about going viral by itself. One video can generate 5 million views and make almost nothing. Another might only reach 100k people but produce thousands of dollars because nearly everyone watching is exactly the type of customer the app was designed for.

这就是为什么我不太在乎「单独走红」。一条视频可以带来 500 万播放却几乎赚不到钱。另一条也许只触达 10 万人，却产生数千美元——因为几乎每个观看者都正好是应用为之设计的那类客户。

The goal isn't views. It's moving people through the entire hierarchy, all the way to retention.

目标不是播放量。而是把人带过整个层级，一路到留存。

When something works, stop trying to be clever

当事情奏效时，别再试图耍聪明

This is one of the easiest mistakes founders make. You finally discover a content format that works, post it five times, get bored, and assume your audience must be bored too.

这是创始人最容易犯的错误之一。你终于发现一种有效的内容格式，发五次，觉得无聊，就假设受众也一定无聊了。

So you abandon the thing that was working and start experimenting with something completely different. But you've seen every post you've published. Most of your audience hasn't.

于是你抛弃正在奏效的东西，开始试验完全不同的东西。但你看过自己发的每一条。你的大多数受众没有。

If one hook consistently generates downloads, make 20 more variations. If screen-recording demos perform well, keep making them. If slideshows work, make more slideshows. Change the opening, the example, the story, the person, or the visuals, but keep the underlying format that has already proven itself.

如果某个钩子持续带来下载，再做 20 个变体。如果录屏演示表现好，继续做。如果幻灯片有效，就做更多幻灯片。换开场、例子、故事、人物或视觉，但保持已经证明有效的底层格式。

Volume is important because you're essentially running experiments. Ten videos might tell you almost nothing. After 50, 100, or 200, the patterns become much easier to see.

量很重要，因为你本质上在跑实验。十条视频可能几乎什么都告诉不了你。到了 50、100 或 200 条，模式就容易看得多。

Certain hooks consistently outperform others. Certain problems get much stronger reactions. Particular demonstrations generate more installs. Some creators convert significantly better than the rest.

某些钩子持续优于其他。某些问题引发强得多的反应。特定演示带来更多安装。某些创作者转化显著好于其余人。

Now you can use a dashboard to compare those results rather than relying on whichever video got the most attention yesterday. The numbers help separate a repeatable acquisition strategy from a lucky viral post.

现在你可以用仪表盘比较这些结果，而不是依赖昨天最受关注的那条视频。数字帮你把可重复的获客策略，与一次幸运的病毒帖区分开。

And once you see the patterns, that's when you multiply yourself.

一旦看到模式，就是你自我放大的时候。

I wouldn't immediately chase influencers with enormous audiences. I'd much rather work with smaller creators who clearly understand how short-form content works.

我不会立刻追拥有巨大受众的网红。我更愿意与明显懂短视频内容的小创作者合作。

Someone with 8,000 followers and several videos reaching 500k views might be dramatically more useful than somebody with 300,000 followers whose audience barely reacts to their posts.

一个有 8000 粉丝、却有几条视频达到 50 万播放的人，可能比一个有 30 万粉丝、受众几乎不回应的人有用得多。

Look for people who understand hooks, explain things clearly, appear natural on camera, and have repeatedly demonstrated that they know how to stop somebody scrolling.

找那些懂钩子、讲解清晰、镜头前自然，并且反复证明自己知道如何让人停下滑动的人。

Then don't ask them to invent your marketing strategy from scratch. Give them the things you've already learned: winning hooks, competitor examples, screen recordings, successful video structures, and the angles that actually generated downloads.

然后别让他们从零发明你的营销策略。把你已经学到的给他们：有效钩子、竞品例子、录屏、成功视频结构，以及真正带来下载的角度。

You want creators producing more variations of something you've already started proving works. That's when distribution begins to feel less like random posting and more like an actual system.

你想要创作者为已经开始证明有效的东西生产更多变体。那时分发开始感觉不像随机发帖，而像真正的系统。

Instead of making five videos yourself, five creators make five each. Then you work with ten creators, then more accounts, until you're testing hundreds of creatives and learning much faster than somebody who posts whenever inspiration strikes.

与其自己做五条视频，不如五个创作者各做五条。然后你与十个创作者合作，再加更多账号，直到你在测试上百个素材，学得比灵感来了才发帖的人快得多。

Those organic winners should tell you what to do next.

那些自然增长的赢家，应该告诉你下一步做什么。

5. PUT MONEY BEHIND WHAT ALREADY WORKS

5. 把钱砸在已经奏效的东西上

Paid acquisition should come after you've learned what people actually respond to. Too many founders do this in the opposite order, building an app, making three random ads, putting $200/day behind them, losing money, and deciding that paid advertising doesn't work.

付费获客应在你了解人们真正会对什么有反应之后。太多创始人顺序反了：做出应用，做三条随机广告，每天砸 200 美元，亏钱，然后判定付费广告没用。

What they really did was pay to test something they could have tested organically first.

他们真正做的，是付钱去测一件本可以先用自然流量测的东西。

If you've already published 100 pieces of content and five of them generated most of your downloads, those five concepts are exactly where I'd start. Keep the same underlying ideas and experiment with different hooks, creators, openings, and product demonstrations.

如果你已经发布了 100 条内容，其中五条贡献了大部分下载，那五个概念正是我会开始的地方。保持相同的底层想法，试验不同钩子、创作者、开场和产品演示。

Paid ads should amplify something that's already working, not rescue something that clearly isn't.

付费广告应放大已经奏效的东西，而不是去救明显不奏效的东西。

Once you start spending money, the business becomes much easier to understand through numbers.

一旦开始花钱，生意就更容易用数字理解。

Imagine you spend $1,000 and acquire 500 installs. Of those 500 people, 50 start a trial and 20 eventually pay. You now have a funnel you can measure and improve.

假设你花 1000 美元获得 500 次安装。这 500 人里，50 人开始试用，20 人最终付费。你现在有了一个可以衡量和改进的漏斗。

Improve your creative and maybe that same $1,000 gets you 700 installs. Improve your App Store page and a larger percentage of visitors download the product. Fix onboarding and maybe 75 people start trials instead of 50.

改进素材，也许同样 1000 美元能带来 700 次安装。改进 App Store 页面，更大比例的访客会下载产品。修好引导，也许 75 人开始试用而不是 50 人。

Improve your paywall and perhaps 30 users become paying customers instead of 20. Improve retention and each customer becomes more valuable, which means you can potentially afford to spend more acquiring the next one.

改进付费墙，也许 30 人成为付费客户而不是 20 人。改进留存，每位客户变得更有价值，意味着你或许能负担更高的获客成本。

This is where things get interesting. Relatively small improvements multiply across the entire funnel, and suddenly you're no longer polishing random screens.

事情在这里变得有意思。相对小的改进会在整个漏斗上倍增，突然间你不再是在打磨随机屏幕。

You're improving the economics of the business.

你在改善生意的经济学。

I'd use Claude Dashboards to make this visible in one place. Ask it to track acquisition costs, install-to-trial conversion, trial-to-paid conversion, revenue, retention, and estimated customer value across campaigns.

我会用 Claude Dashboards 把这些放在一个地方看清。让它跟踪获客成本、安装到试用转化、试用到付费转化、收入、留存，以及跨活动的估计客户价值。

Then compare different periods and cohorts to understand which improvements actually made a difference. If you're tracking the data correctly, you can see whether your recent onboarding changes improved conversion or whether you're simply getting a different mix of users.

然后比较不同时期和队列，理解哪些改进真正起了作用。如果数据跟踪正确，你能看到最近的引导改动是改善了转化，还是你只是拿到了不同的用户组合。

You can also inspect the queries and refresh dates behind the charts. That matters because a dashboard full of impressive-looking numbers is useless if the metrics are defined incorrectly or the data is stale.

你也可以检查图表背后的查询和刷新日期。这很重要，因为一堆看起来很炫的数字，如果指标定义错误或数据过时，就毫无用处。

For deeper questions, Claude Dashboards can hand the work over to tools like Amplitude, Mixpanel, or PostHog. You don't need to replace your existing analytics stack just to make the information easier to explore.

对更深的问题，Claude Dashboards 可以把工作交给 Amplitude、Mixpanel 或 PostHog 这类工具。你不必为了更容易探索信息而替换现有分析栈。

The dashboard gives you visibility, but you still have to make the decisions.

仪表盘给你可见性，但决策仍得你来做。

Now improve the product

现在改进产品

I would only spend significantly more time expanding the actual product once I had meaningful usage to work with. At that point, you're no longer guessing what customers might want because people are already showing you.

只有当我有了有意义的使用量可工作时，我才会花显著更多时间扩展实际产品。那时你不再猜测客户可能想要什么，因为人们已经在展示给你看。

Read the App Store reviews, support messages, refund requests, and cancellation reasons. Look at where customers disappear, what your best users keep returning to, and which features people repeatedly ask for.

读 App Store 评价、客服消息、退款请求和取消原因。看客户在哪里消失，你最好的用户反复回来做什么，以及人们反复要求哪些功能。

Don't automatically turn every feature request into something that needs to be built. Keep asking whether it makes the core promise of the app stronger.

别自动把每个功能请求变成必须构建的东西。持续问：它是否让应用的核心承诺更强。

Sometimes the right decision is adding a new feature. Sometimes it's deleting three screens. Sometimes it's helping users reach their first result 30 seconds faster.

有时正确决策是加新功能。有时是删掉三屏。有时是帮用户快 30 秒到达第一个结果。

Maybe the default experience isn't right, the quality of the output needs improving, or one part of onboarding is confusing enough to make people abandon the whole thing.

也许默认体验不对，输出质量需要提升，或引导的某一部分足够令人困惑，以至于人们放弃整件事。

The goal is to improve conversion, revenue, retention, or the actual outcome people downloaded your app to achieve. It's not to win an award for having the longest list of features.

目标是改善转化、收入、留存，或人们下载应用想达成的实际结果。不是去赢「功能列表最长」奖。

And because Claude Dashboards can keep your most important metrics in one place, you can make product decisions while looking at the actual business consequences. Does improving that feature increase repeat usage? Are users who complete a particular action more likely to subscribe? Does a change improve retention across multiple cohorts or only one?

而且因为 Claude Dashboards 可以把最重要的指标放在一处，你可以一边看着实际生意后果一边做产品决策。改进那个功能会增加重复使用吗？完成某特定动作的用户是否更可能订阅？某项改动是改善了多个队列的留存，还是只有一个？

These are much better questions than simply asking AI what feature you should build next.

这些问题比单纯问 AI「下一步该做什么功能」好得多。

Once you know what works, scaling becomes almost boring. If one creator consistently produces paying customers, give them more work. If one format keeps generating downloads, make 30 more versions. If one ad remains profitable, gradually scale it and create more variations around the same concept.

一旦知道什么有效，放大几乎变得无聊。如果某个创作者持续带来付费客户，就给他们更多工作。如果某种格式持续带来下载，就再做 30 个版本。如果某条广告仍然盈利，就逐步放量，并围绕同一概念做更多变体。

If one audience converts twice as well as another, go deeper into that audience and figure out why.

如果某个受众的转化是另一个的两倍，就更深进入那个受众并弄清为什么。

People imagine scaling a business means constantly discovering new growth hacks. In reality, a huge part of it is finding something that works and refusing to stop doing it.

人们想象放大生意意味着不断发现新增长黑客。现实中，很大一部分是找到奏效的东西，并拒绝停止去做。

THE WHOLE FORMULA

整套公式

I used Claude Dashboards to study 268,117 apps, but the final method isn't particularly complicated. The same fundamental patterns kept appearing across successful products, and if I had to compress the entire process into five lessons, this would be it.

我用 Claude Dashboards 研究了 268,117 个应用，但最终方法并不特别复杂。同样的基本模式在成功产品上反复出现；如果要把整个过程压成五条教训，就是这些。

1. Enter a proven market. Find an annoying or painful problem that people already spend money solving. You don't need to invent a new category, and multiple competitors making serious money is usually validation rather than a reason to give up.

1. 进入已被验证的市场。找到人们已经在花钱解决的烦人/痛苦问题。你不必发明新品类；多个竞争对手在认真赚钱，通常是验证，而不是放弃的理由。

2. Build in 7 days, not 7 months. Study the winning products and understand the decisions behind them. Give Shipper a clear plan, screenshots, and references, build the onboarding, paywall, and core loop, then iterate until the result no longer feels like another AI-generated clone.

2. 用 7 天构建，而不是 7 个月。研究赢家产品并理解其背后决策。给 Shipper 清晰计划、截图和参考，做出引导、付费墙和核心循环，然后迭代到结果不再像又一个 AI 生成克隆。

3. Treat onboarding as part of marketing. A download isn't a customer. People still need to feel understood, experience enough value to want the result, and have a reason to pay. Track where they drop off, and keep testing.

3. 把引导当作营销的一部分。下载不是客户。人们仍需要感到被理解，体验到足够价值以至于想要结果，并有理由付费。跟踪他们在哪里流失，并持续测试。

4. Test distribution at stupid volume. Study formats that already work, make your own variations, and measure what happens beyond the view count. Optimize for views → downloads → trials → revenue → retention, not simply for attention.

4. 以夸张的量测试分发。研究已经奏效的格式，做自己的变体，并衡量播放数以外发生了什么。优化播放 → 下载 → 试用 → 收入 → 留存，而不只是注意力。

5. Scale whatever survives the entire funnel. Work with more winning creators, make more variations of proven creatives, increase paid spend behind profitable concepts, and keep improving conversion and retention. Repeat the things that actually move the business forward.

5. 放大能穿过整个漏斗存活下来的东西。与更多赢家创作者合作，为已验证素材做更多变体，增加盈利概念上的付费投放，并持续改善转化与留存。重复真正推动生意前进的事。

That's really the formula. The 268,117 apps are useful because they give you something more valuable than another random startup idea: patterns you can examine and learn from.

这就是公式。268,117 个应用有用，是因为它们给你的东西，比又一个随机创业点子更有价值：你可以审视和学习的模式。

And Claude Dashboards makes those patterns much easier to work with. You can turn large datasets into understandable charts, compare the numbers that matter, and keep watching what happens as the business changes instead of depending on gut feeling.

而 Claude Dashboards 让这些模式好用得多。你可以把大数据集变成可理解的图表，比较真正重要的数字，并在生意变化时持续观察，而不是依赖直觉。

But the dashboard isn't going to build a successful app for you. Successful products usually aren't created from one magical idea or one perfect AI prompt. They're built by making a long sequence of fairly simple decisions correctly, then testing them far more times than everybody else.

但仪表盘不会替你做出成功应用。成功产品通常不是来自一个神奇点子或一个完美 AI 提示。它们来自把一长串相当简单的决策做对，然后比其他所有人测试更多次。

选一个人们已经在付钱的市场。研究赢家并理解它们为何奏效。做出最小有用版本，在做完之前就开始分发，并跟随数字，从第一次曝光一路到数月后仍在付费的客户。
